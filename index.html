Abstract

Recent video generation models synthesize high-quality short clips (e.g., 5–15 s), yet autonomous film generation remains challenging. Given a synopsis, an autonomous filmmaking system must coordinate screenplay development, character and scene design, and shot composition while maintaining consistency throughout a long-form film (e.g., three minutes). Since individual clips are generated stochastically, narrative coherence and cross-shot consistency depend on the harness that coordinates planning, synthesis, verification, and regeneration.

We present FilmHarness, a training-free, model-agnostic agentic harness for autonomous film generation. It establishes hierarchical typed contracts that specify narrative and visual constraints before content generation, compiles them into a dependency-aware task graph that decouples narrative shots from model-specific generation windows, and integrates multi-scale verification from keyframe pairs to the complete film. Verification feedback identifies the keyframes or video segments responsible for detected inconsistencies and guides selective regeneration of the affected units and their downstream dependents.

To ensure fair comparison, we evaluate FilmHarness alongside four multi-agent systems and two general coding agents using shared image and video foundation models with identical generation parameters. Across eight synopses with Wan2.2 and MiniMax H3, FilmHarness ranks first on 8 and 4 of 12 film-level metrics, respectively, and within the top two on 10 metrics with each backbone. Blind pairwise human evaluation yields preference rates of 88% for character consistency and 72% for overall quality after excluding ties. Ablation studies further show that reference conditioning improves identity preservation, verification reduces visual discontinuities, and explicit shot planning improves framing accuracy.

Method

FilmHarness treats film generation as harness design with language, image, vision-language and video foundation models.

Contract-first planning

The planner writes the story, screenplay, scene contracts and shot designs level by level. Review boards check each level and revise only the flagged units, then the plan and its identity, wardrobe and location references are locked.

Window-decoupled compilation

Each shot is split into micro-clips that fit the generation window of the video model. Adjacent micro-clips share a boundary keyframe, and each keyframe pair keeps faces, wardrobe, props, set and grade fixed.

Multi-scale verification

Keyframes, pairs, clips, shots, scenes, reels and the film are verified in turn. A rejection names the responsible units, and only those units and their dependents are regenerated.

Comparison with Baselines

All methods use the same synopses, image and video foundation models, and generation parameters. The four adapted multi-agent systems also share language and reviewer models. Codex and Claude Code each develop their own workflow. Sampling strategies remain method-specific, with generation costs reported in the paper.
